## Description

SPHEREx Space is a free web tool that plays real images from NASA's SPHEREx space telescope as a moving picture, so anyone can see how a patch of sky changes and spot what moves.

Try it, no account needed: https://www.spherex.space

What the video shows (every picture is a screen recording of the live tool on real SPHEREx data):
0:00 The whole sky on a globe, in every wavelength: star map, visible, near-infrared, infrared, SPHEREx.
0:02 Pick Barnard's Star from the catalogue and zoom from the whole sky down to it.
0:06 The lab plays the real SPHEREx passes: Barnard's Star steps along its path while the background stands still.
0:12 Overlays off, then on: our searches mark what moved.
0:15 The same star in every named band and in each of the six detectors.
0:20 Click any dot: its passport and its spectrum.
0:24 Find Planet X: Eris found blind, 95.6 ± 0.4 au from three passes linked (JPL 95.5).

SPHEREx maps the whole sky in 102 infrared colours between 0.75 and 5 micrometres and comes back to every place about every six months. Raw frames show colour flicker, not motion, so we clean, stack and colour every pass, then play the passes like a blink comparator and search them for movers. Every find is a lead with its evidence, never a discovery.

Challenge: Planet X and SPHEREx, NASA International Space Apps Challenge 2026.
Team: Good Karma Lab, Barcelona Local Event. Aleksandr Ostapenko (team owner), Katherine Karmatskikh.

Data credits: NASA SPHEREx Level-2 spectral images (DR1 and QR3), NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive (IRSA); ESA Gaia DR3; NASA/Goddard SVS Deep Star Maps; DSS2, 2MASS and AllWISE sky surveys; JPL Small-Body Database and Horizons; SIMBAD (CDS). This work uses SPHEREx data products (JPL and Caltech, funded by NASA). Independent project, not endorsed by NASA.
