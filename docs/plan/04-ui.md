# 4. User interface

Four screens: **Globe** (the atlas, the way in), **Lab** (one field: pictures, films, searches, object, spectrum), **Findings** (the five
test targets and the leads a person saved), **Method** (how everything is computed, generated from the code's constants). The look:
dark glass panels, teal edges, amber for motion, white for saturation; interface text in plain English.

## 4.1 Interaction rules (requirements)

1. **Status only on the picture.** One short line on or under the picture: what is shown ("Pass 2 of 3 · Mar 2026 · colour"), what is
   loading ("reading 366 of 592 exposures"), what a search found ("1 track · 1 pair"). Never a sentence about what a view means or where
   a feature is.
2. **No explanatory paragraphs.** Panels hold values and controls; the explanation is the control's tooltip or a link to its Method
   section.
3. **The Display panel is never collapsed** and sits high in the Controls tab (under the view switch and the chips, above the
   searches), as a grid of one-line switches. The whole Controls tab fits 1000×620 without a screen of scrolling. Nothing used often
   sits behind a closed disclosure or at the bottom.
4. **No layout jumps.** Every panel and line reserves its space; late data fills reserved space; a long status is cut with an
   ellipsis, never wrapped into new lines; a mode, band, size or tab change never moves other controls.
5. **A switch does what it says,** in every view: Marker, Trajectory, Mover rings, Anomalies, Speed rings each govern only their own
   layer; all off = nothing drawn on the picture.
6. **Nothing waits silently.** Every action shows progress from the first moment (phase words from the service, a bar that moves); a
   control that cannot work says why in its own label ("up to 10′").
7. **The picture first.** The quick white frame appears within seconds, colour as soon as two passes are complete, refinement behind.
8. **One source, one ring, one label.** Labels never overprint (one label queue with priorities: name > lead number > ring speed; a
   label that would leave the picture flips side).
9. **State in the address.** `#/lab?ra=…&dec=…&fov=…&view=…&time=…&bands=…&sel=…` — every view opens by a link; the address writes RA to
   0.01 s and Dec to 0.1″ so the warm cache is hit.
10. **Leaving does not cancel.** Switching size or field leaves the old job running at low priority; coming back attaches to it.
11. **Phone width (375 px):** no horizontal overflow; panels stack under the picture.
12. **Every display setting is in the address** (lesson of WiseView, Backyard Worlds' blink tool): place, size, view, time mode, bands,
    pass, speed, stretch, overlays, selection — a lead is shared by sending the link.
13. **A static context image beside the blink** (WiseView keeps a still optical image under the flashing one to reject artefacts): the
    archive image of the place (DSS or Pan-STARRS) can be shown as a small still panel next to the moving picture.
14. **Stretch controls** worth having in Display (WiseView): the asinh softness ("linear" parameter) and a cap for bright pixels (fixed,
    percentile or adaptive), defaults unchanged; each stretch change goes through the frame audit before it becomes a default.

- WebGL globe (orthographic) and Mollweide map; HiPS tiles through the service proxy (Allsky image at order 3, real tiles from order

- **Open** always opens the Lab **by coordinates** (the target's place at epoch 2025.9), never "an object".

- **Picture area:** one square WebGL canvas (centred, never stretched), the zoom inset in the corner farthest from the object, an
  overlay canvas for marks. The status line and the timeline belong to the picture; the timeline appears only on the Controls tab.
- **Timeline:** one dot per pass (and per archive image when "Archive images" is on); clicking a date label moves the slider; the
  player steps through passes; Difference shows pass vs first; Co-add shows the stack of all passes.
- **Controls tab:** place (in the header), view, time mode, band chips (named bands; in the Wall of detectors the chips become
  D1–D6 with ranges), **Display grid (open)**, searches. Every explanation is a tooltip.
- **Loading:** the status line shows the phases: "finding the exposures" → "reading the quick subset" → "pass 1 of 3: reading 40 of
  143" → "colour for 2 of 3 passes" → "searching for movers: linking" → done. A second line is never added; the Movers row carries the
  search progress with its own bar.

## 4.10 Method page

Generated sections (one per algorithm) with the parameters read from the engine's constants, the validation numbers read from the
latest validation report, the videos and pictures of the validation evidence, a status table (what the application computes today) and
a change log; one consistent account in "we"; no proposals, no history of mistakes (state the rule and its reason).
