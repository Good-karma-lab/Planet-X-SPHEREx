# 2.5 Searches

Things in the sky move in three ways and each needs its own search; a fourth kind of find is a change of brightness in place.

| What moves | How it looks in SPHEREx data | Search | Kind of find |
|---|---|---|---|
| nearby stars, brown dwarfs | arcseconds per year: a shift between passes, in every stack | between passes (2.5.2) | shift between passes |
| asteroids, comets | arcminutes per day: a new place every visit, in no stack | inside a pass, the film search (2.5.4) | track inside a pass |
| fast near-Earth bodies | arcminutes per hour: crosses a field in hours | inside a visit (2.5.5) | track inside a visit |
| bodies beyond Neptune | a few ″/day in a pass, a jump of tens of ′ between passes, too faint for one exposure | exposures added along the motion (2.5.6–2.5.9) | distant body |
| a pair or variable in place | the peak of a blend hops; flux changes at a fixed place | pairs (2.5.3) | change of brightness in place |

Common rules: nothing about any object is given to a search (a named object only decides the field centre); a catalogue check comes
**after** the search; every search has a chance control (2.6.3); a find is a lead with its evidence.

## 2.5.1 Sources of a field (the click list and the input of the searches)

Local maxima of the white stack of each pass smoothed to a point source (7″ FWHM), ≥ 5σ over the local background for the list (S/N ≥ 7
for a click to select it, saturated always), inside a halo above the halo by the contrast rule (2.2.6), only where the pass has data;
a saturated zone (≥ one detector pixel) is one source at its centre; two peaks closer than ~8″ are one source. Linked pass to pass;
each source carries its place at epoch 2025.9 (straight-line fit when it moves), per-pass place/S/N/flux (detected or measured at the
expected place; `cov: false` where the pass has no data), `moves` (straight line in time, shift over the baseline ≥ 1″ and ≥ 4σ with
σ per pass = hypot(0.3″, 3″/S/N)), `changes` (fluxes differ by ≥ 5σ and a factor 1.3, with a 7 % calibration floor), `edge` (within 20″
of the grid border). At most 4000 sources, saturated first, then by S/N. Gaia completeness (G < 16 within 3″): Barnard 5′ 75 %, 10′
88 %, 20′ 89 % (G < 14: 97 %); misses are stars within ~8″ of a brighter one and stars in the glare of a halo.

## 2.5.2 Search between passes (years)

On the white stacks of a field with ≥ 3 passes, started automatically when the field is final.

```
sources per pass: peaks of gauss(stack, 0.7·σ_psf) (FWHM 7″) in a 3·FWHM box, sm − halo > 8·MAD and > 3·hl,
                  covered by all passes and 20″ in from the edge; a saturated zone = one source at its centre
stationary: a source with a source within 2.5″ in every other pass
tracks: first and last pass fix v (3–120 ″/yr); predicted places in the passes between; a source within 2.5″ of the prediction in
        ≥ 1 of them with brightness within a factor 2.5 → a track of ≥ 3 passes; v refitted on the members; tracks through the same
        sources merged
```

**After the search, checks on every track** (each track carries a `check` state):

| State | Rule | Shown |
|---|---|---|
| `same` | a Gaia DR3 star with pm > 2 mas/yr, moved to each pass date, within 3″ of the track in every pass | drawn, "Gaia: same motion", named |
| `still` | a Gaia star within 6″ with proper motion far below the track's rate: the shift is a centroid effect | rejected: counted, not drawn |
| blend | the peak hops between two cores while the light-weighted centroid in a fixed window moves < 40 % of the hop and < 3″, with a second peak within 12″ or an elongated blob | rejected as a mover, becomes a pair find (2.5.3) |
| `none` | no Gaia star within 6″ | drawn, "no Gaia motion" (unexplained ≠ new: a brown dwarf like ε Indi B, a distant body or an artefact) |
| `pending` | no catalogue answered (re-checked at most once a minute; fallback through the SIMBAD/Gaia identify path) | drawn dashed, "not checked yet" |
| `weak` | only the minimum 3 passes, S/N < 15 and not `same` | counted, not drawn (until the chance control gives a probability) |

Validation: Barnard 20′ ~343–470 sources per pass, ~460 stationary, **one track** 9.98–10.46 ″/yr toward 352–353° (Gaia names
Barnard's Star, positions 0.7″ from Gaia's prediction); Barnard 5′ by coordinates only: one track 10.5″/yr, PA 353°; ε Indi 20′: two
tracks (B at 4.52 ″/yr toward 128°, unexplained by Gaia; saturated A at 5.4–5.9 ″/yr, `same`); ε Indi 5′: one track 4.5″/yr through all
four passes; RW Aurigae 10′: the G 12.6 "mover" of 6.6″/yr (Gaia pm 0.2 mas/yr) rejected and delivered as a pair.

Limits: < 3 passes gives no search; < 3″ or > 120″/yr not linked; a shift smaller than ~5″ over the baseline is not reliably caught; a
source on a diffraction spike can still link when the roll angle repeats; a body seen in two passes only is not a track.

## 2.5.3 Pairs changing in place (fourth kind of find)

Two sources the picture shows as one elongated blob can make a "mover": the brighter core changes between passes and the peak jumps
(RW Aurigae's neighbourhood: a saturated star and TYC 2389-429-1, 5.7″ apart; peak jumps ~8″, centroid within 0.6″).

```
from a rejected blend track, or from the source list: two visible sources 3.5–12″ apart, one with changes.flag
fit two Gaussian components (FWHM 10″) at fixed places to the stacks of every pass and detector
real pair only if: both components measured in every pass with data at flux/err ≥ 10, each ≥ 15 at least once,
                   and the ratio of the two changes by ≥ 4σ of its own error between passes
verdict: "steady" (< 10 % ratio change) | "one component varies (same change in every detector, spread < 0.15)"
         | "change depends on the detector (a variable component with a colour, or a different wavelength mix)"
```

Card: verdict, flux of each core per pass, ratio per pass, change by detector, SIMBAD/Gaia names of both cores, crops of every pass, a
zoom of 4–12×. Validation: RW Aurigae 10′ and 20′: one pair (separation 5.7″, ratio 0.67 → 0.85 → 0.96, 4.4σ, "change depends on the
detector", component 2 = TYC 2389-429-1); the 12 "pairs" of the ecliptic field and the others of the warm set fail the rule (a source
that appears in one pass next to a steady neighbour). Open: a chance control for pairs (2.6.3).

## 2.5.4 Search inside a pass: the film search (days)

On the film's frames (2.3.10), detectors D1–D3 (a detector is used only if its visits have ≥ 5 exposures of other visits ≥ 0.2 d away).

```
candidates: peaks of the motion maps ≥ 5σ outside the static and halo masks, brighter than 0.74 × the static sky there
verify (on − off PSF photometry in the exposures, OUTLIER kept):
   stage 1 at the detection place: positive in every exposure of its visit and ≥ 2σ → stage 2
   stage 2: place free within 0.6 px per exposure, references at the fitted place; off = median of 9 nearest in λ (within 12 %) at
            the same place in exposures of the same detector ≥ 0.2 d from the visit, from every pass; verified at ≥ 4σ
   at most the 600 strongest candidates verified; a control measures 40″ aside of every 8th candidate
link (orbit model):
   observer = Earth (ERFA epv00) + spacecraft (X_SC, Y_SC, Z_SC)
   pairs of candidates of different visits, 3–200 ″/h; grid uniform in 1/D, D 0.4–6 au (step 0.04 in 1/D), dD −48…48 km/s (step 4)
   Kepler orbit through the two places; keep 0.8 < a < 8 au, e < 0.6; members = candidates within 5″ of the prediction under ONE
   hypothesis; a member farther than 10″ after the group fit is dropped; a source belongs to one track
   linkable candidates: positive in every exposure and ≥ 2σ; a track needs ≥ 5σ together (Σ significance / sqrt(n))
recover: visits where the body was not found, at the orbit's place (where all fitting orbits agree within 3″): motion map ≥ 3.5σ
         within 1.5 grid points; a line track (not taken over by an orbit) needs ≥ 4 found visits
straight-line link (fallback/merge): 3–200 ″/h within 4″, scatter ≤ 1.5″; only the 1000 strongest candidates (the link is quadratic)
control: candidates moved to chance places (times and visits kept), linked and recovered the same way, ≥ 12–20 trials; a track is
         drawn only when chance makes < 0.15 tracks of its length per field
check: IMCCE SkyBoT (a known body within 30″ at the middle visit, rate within 40 %); no known body → lead
distance: from the bend of the path, an interval (not a measurement); a and e are not shown (not determined by a 1.5–3 day arc)
```

Validation: ecliptic field RA 169.8917, Dec +4.3514, 60′, pass of December 2025 (24 visits, 163 exposures, 6″ grid): **4 of 4 known
bodies** — (9952) 1991 AK n 10, 33σ; (9078) 1994 PB2 n 10, 14σ; (11020) Orwell n 6, 12σ; (43959) 1997 CB26 n 4, 13σ; SkyBoT within
1.6–3.5″; control 0 of 50 verified at chance places; chance tracks of ≥ 4 visits 0.0 in 12 trials. Offline at 1°: distances
2.46 / 2.17 / 2.73 / 2.79 au against JPL 2.28 / 1.98 / 2.84 / 3.21 (4–13 %; all inside the fit interval); residual from the orbit
1.0–1.9″ (straight line 5.8–9.5″). ε Indi B, Barnard, WISE 0855, RW Aur 60′: 0 tracks; R Draconis 60′: one unexplained 5-visit orbit
track (14.3″/h, 8.7σ) — **to be checked before it is called anything** (it also appears at 440 px; not at 400 px). Unexplained tracks in
ε Indi B and R Draconis changed with every setting tried: the mark of chance.

Limits: no tracks fainter than V ≈ 19 (one or two verified visits); a comet on a parabola or a body beyond 6 au is not linked; the
time sampling is the survey's (a few visits a day, gaps of days); the 6″ grid that links (43959) produces halo candidates around a very
bright star — the halo mask (equivalent radius ≥ 150″, grown 0.4 r) handles ε Indi 60′; a per-field grid choice is the production rule.

## 2.5.5 Search inside a visit (hours)

For fields ≤ 10′, on the exposures of each visit (exposures < 0.5 day apart), real pixels on the field grid.

```
1 detections per exposure (scaled by its brightness against the visit's median on static stars):
   static = pixelwise median of the scaled exposures; z = (scaled − static)/sqrt(σ² + (0.15·static)²) in [−5, 12]
   matched 3×3 mean > 5.5 × its robust scatter (raised until the negative image has ≤ 1 peak); peaks closer than 3 px are one
   saturated region (pixels within 2 px) = one detection at its centre; none within 4 px + 4 × its radius
2 stars removed: a Gaia star (G ≤ 17.5 at the epoch) within 0.9 px (3 px for a saturated region); a place detected within 1.2 px in
   ≥ 3 exposures spread over ≥ min(3 days, 0.4 of the time span) — except detections > 8× brighter than the faintest quarter of
   that cluster (a mover passing over a faint static source); a saturated region at one place at two times ≥ 0.1 d apart
3 linking per visit: every pair of detections in different exposures defines a line; collect detections within 1.0 px at their own
   time (one per exposure); best line by Σ min(S/N, 100) with ≥ 5 exposures; refit, remove, repeat (≤ 8 tracks per visit);
   accept only if: scatter ≤ 0.7 px; length ≥ max(0.5, 2.4/sqrt(median S/N/5)) px; 0.5–800 px/day; detections in ≥ 45 % of the
   exposures covering the line; burst consistency (inside each burst of ≥ 3 detections < 6 min apart the shift agrees with the line
   within 0.25 px + 2.5 × burst scatter × sqrt(12/n) + 25 % of the expected shift); proved by a measured burst shift, or ≥ 4 epochs
   (gaps > 14 min), or a bright tight line (median S/N ≥ 50, scatter ≤ 0.6 px, ≥ 8 detections); a weaker track within 10 px at a
   rate within 50 % of a stronger one is a companion (dropped); a member far from the line fitted to the others is dropped
   parallax: for the best seed lines, detections moved to the geocentre for trial distances 0.08, 0.15, 0.3, 0.6, 1.2, 2.5 au
   (observed − geocentric = spacecraft position projected / distance); adopted only if it gains ≥ 2 members or lowers the scatter
   by 30 % (≥ 8 members); acceptance runs in that frame (433 Eros at 0.46 au needs it; the wrong sign does not find it)
4 known bodies: IMCCE SkyBoT cones at the median epoch of each group (≤ 0.5 d), moved to the spacecraft viewpoint; V ≤ 21 and
   uncertainty ≤ 120″; a body opened by name also from JPL Horizons (observer 500@-163182); a track is that body when it lies within
   2.5 px (5 px Horizons) in ≥ half of its detections; unknown candidates only after every known body is asked
5 chance: exposure times permuted inside each visit (positions and brightness kept), the whole linker re-run (≥ 100 trials per
   field); publish a track only when its false-alarm probability < 1 %
```

**Validation gate (must pass before the search is switched on by default):** the eight known bodies (4 Vesta, 1 Ceres, 2 Pallas, 7 Iris,
45 Eugenia, 433 Eros, 3I/ATLAS, C/2025 A6 Lemmon) on 10′ cut-outs, truth = Horizons from the spacecraft, found = rate within 10 % and
median offset < 10″: the first search found **7 of 8** (all but Lemmon, a diffuse coma found only by the stack along its
ephemeris); a second implementation without the burst, parallax and far-member rules found 4 of 8 and was left off. Target: **≥ 7 of
8**, and 0 published tracks on Barnard 10′, RW Aurigae 10′, ε Indi B 10′. Completeness (synthetic movers on the Eugenia field, 6 trials
each): at 27″/h peak S/N 8/15/30: 4/6/6 of 6; at 60″/h: 1/3/4; at 120″/h: 0/1/1. False tracks from time shuffles: < 0.1 per visit (95 %
upper limit 0.2–0.3), a lower bound (correlated artefacts are not shuffled away).

**Stack along an ephemeris (a named body):** the motion-only z maps read at the predicted place + trial offsets (≤ 8 px), summed
`T = Σz/√N` over offsets covered by ≥ 60 % of exposures; significant when higher than the peaks of all 40 control paths (the path moved by
15–40 px, wrapped; p ≈ 0.02). Completeness by injected point sources on control paths (20 trials per level). Detection test only, not
astrometry for a saturated body.

Limits: a single exposure reaches V ~16–17; fast faint movers are found in fewer than half of the trials at some rates; tracks are not
checked in detector coordinates (fixed-detector artefacts move ~75,000 px/day on the sky, far above the 800 px/day cap).

## 2.5.6 Bodies beyond Neptune: shared products

Made once per field and pass, reused by the one-pass search, the deep sum and the browser's distance slider:

```
every exposure of D1–D3 (D4 as a separate product) on a 3.075″ grid; background ZODI, median in 40 wavelength bins, clipped median;
   flagged pixels dropped except OUTLIER; each pixel spread with 1.85″, image smoothed to 3.2″
visit image: median of its exposures (groups ≥ 3); a pair → the lower value; a single exposure → points > 5σ off left out
static sky[det] = clipped (3σ) mean of the visits of the same detector in ALL OTHER passes
difference = visit − static, clipped at ±5σ (one visit cannot make a detection)
mask: static > 4σ with a margin growing with brightness (2 grid points at 4σ … 10 at 4000σ); costs 19–25 % of the field
```

## 2.5.7 Bodies beyond Neptune: one pass and the distance slider

```
hypothesis: heliocentric distance d (default 30–300 au) and own motion μ (|μ| ≤ escape speed 5016″/day / d^1.5)
grid uniform in 1/d and μ so neighbours differ by ≤ 3″ in any visit; coverage ≥ 60 % of the weight
parallax path: X0 on the line of sight at distance d, seen from Earth (ERFA) + spacecraft in every visit; off = P + μ(t − t0)
sum: Σ w·diff(x + off) / sqrt(Σw), w = 1/σ²; divided again by the sum's own robust spread (the static-sky noise is shared: the formal
     error was 3–26 % too low)
noise ceiling: the same on the negated differences; candidates = peaks > 1.05 × the best negated peak (≥ 5σ); a ridge along a body's
     track is dropped
hypothesis count estimated before the grid is built (within 2–18 %); cap 10⁶ (~13 min); if the asked nearest distance exceeds the
     cap, the job raises it in 5 % steps until it fits and says so in one line ("nearest distance 30 → 34.7 au")
refine: bright candidates' place and μ tuned on the unclipped differences (fractional reads)
photometry: PSF flux along the track in every exposure of the pass minus the median at the same sky place in the 5 nearest-in-λ
     exposures of the other passes (same detector); confirmed: ≥ 5σ, positive in every detector with ≥ 4 points, track ≥ 12″
control: the same on 4 tracks 1′ aside; distance reported as an interval (where S/N is within 1 of the best)
a candidate moving < 2 px over the pass is a change of brightness, not a body
```

**The slider** (the moving picture of this case): the browser receives the visit differences (one byte each) and the per-visit offsets for
48 distances; it shifts and adds them for the chosen distance and own motion (WebGL, or CPU for small fields); a point comes into focus
at the body's distance and smears beyond it; "Show the noise" does the same on the negated differences; the peaks of the scan are listed
and a click sets the sliders.

Validation: Eris (RA 27.378, Dec −0.120, 18′, July 2025, D1–D3, 315 exposures of 3 passes, 18 visits): found blind 12.0σ against a
ceiling 6.7σ, 96.4 au (52–182; JPL 95.5), 2″ from JPL, photometry 0.072 ± 0.008 mJy (8.7σ; D1 0.131 > D2 0.068 > D3 0.040: icy colour),
control 2.1, 0.6, 0.2, −0.6σ; scan 274 s on 6 threads, 3.6 × 10⁵ hypotheses, service responsive (health < 4 ms). All three passes:
12.0 / 13.3 / 13.5σ against 6.7 / 8.0 / 6.5σ (offline 11.5 / 14.0 / 13.5σ against 6.5–6.8σ), intervals all contain 95.5 au.
Depth: 5σ along a known track 0.020–0.070 mJy per pass by field; blind limit 0.045–0.065 mJy per pass (V ≈ 19 for Eris's colour, ≈ 20
for Sedna's).

## 2.5.8 Bodies beyond Neptune: the deep sum of all passes

```
hypothesis = an orbit: a sky point of the middle pass at its middle visit, distance d, own motion μ, zero radial speed;
   carried to every visit of every pass as a two-body orbit, seen from the telescope; only orbits that stay inside every pass's field
two levels: coarse μ (3″ over a pass) fixes the track inside each pass (visits added once per coarse step); fine μ (4.5″ shift in the
   outer passes) shifts and adds the pass images
stage 1: S/N of the sum per orbit, best per sky point; the same for the negated data
stage 2: PSF photometry along the orbit in every exposure of all passes (minus the same sky place in the other passes) for the 100
   strongest peaks of the sum AND the 100 strongest of the negated sum (negated fluxes negated); place, d, μ tuned first
threshold = 1.05 × the best negated peak in photometry; a candidate needs positive flux in every pass
one source, one candidate: peaks sharing a track in any pass within 20″ are one (keep the strongest)
the photometry of a track re-measures every reference exposure: cache those measurements
```

The slowest job (measured: 11 min sum + 15 min photometry on 8 processes for an 18′ field; 18–32 min for 1° at 250–2000 au; 2 min with
the Planet Nine prior). It starts only for fields with ≥ 3 passes and says how long it will take before it starts (plan call).
Validation: **Sedna (V 20.8) found blind**: 4.4 million orbits per sky point (75–92 au), 6.6σ in the sum under a ceiling of 8.1σ;
photometry 0.031 ± 0.003 mJy, 9.1σ against a threshold 6.8σ (best negated 6.5σ); 82.9 au (JPL 83.0); track 3.2–3.7″ from JPL; control
0 of 8. Eris 20.3σ in the sum, 0.100 ± 0.005 mJy (20.9σ), 95.5 au, track 0.4–0.8″ from JPL. Blind limit ~0.034 mJy (V ≈ 20.9 for
Sedna's colour, 19.7 for Eris's). Note: the three Sedna fields were centred where Sedna was in each pass ("blind" inside an 18′ area); a
search with no prior place needs the outer passes over a ~1° strip.

## 2.5.9 Link of passes, the jump, D4 and the WISE archive check

- **Jump:** half a year apart SPHEREx sees a place from the other side of Earth's orbit (2 au), so a body at d jumps ~2 au/d: 72′ at 95
  au, 14′ at 500 au; the size of the jump is the distance. **Link:** one two-body orbit through the tracks of two passes (six free
  numbers; speed below escape; misfit ≤ 6″ and a closed orbit = one body); the orbit predicts the third pass, a candidate there within 2′
  confirms. Validation: Eris 95.89 ± 0.54 au (JPL 95.5), Makemake 52.38 ± 0.23 au (JPL 52.3); predicted third-pass place 45″ / 57″ from
  the blind detection. The shape of the orbit is not determined (semi-major axis of Eris 67 / 275 / 82 au in three fits).
- **Jump corridor** (designed, never run): for a candidate nearer than ~200 au the next-pass place is outside the field; read a corridor
  of ±5′ along the line of places allowed by the distance. Built and tested in M13 before it is offered.
- **D4 alone and the Planet Nine prior:** D4 (2.4–3.8 µm) only, orbits prograde with inclination 5–30° and 0.4–0.9 of escape speed
  (`cos ψ = cos i / cos b` fixes the motion direction); limit from 30 artificial bodies per field read on their own orbits (injection
  calibrated so the photometry returns the injected flux: ratio 0.999 in D4). Validation: blind limit 0.039 mJy clean field, 0.047 near
  the ecliptic, 0.042 with the prior (WISE Planet Nine search: 0.065 mJy) = 1.4–1.7× deeper; the prior cuts orbits 12× and time 10× but
  gains only 11 % in depth.
- **WISE archive check (on request):** NEOWISE exposures of 2014–2024 along the candidate's orbit (about 900 cutouts, ~1 min each from
  IRSA: run in the background, cached by candidate): epoch image = median of its exposures; static = clipped mean of the other epochs;
  only static sources above ~0.1 mJy masked; flux scale from CatWISE stars (4 % scatter); sum along the orbit scanning its uncertainty,
  792 shifted places as the noise reference. Validation: the candidate at 394 au (RA 60.94, Dec +8.39, Aug 2025; 0.034 ± 0.006 mJy in
  D4) gives −0.0005 ± 0.0031 mJy in WISE where a body of its flux would stand at 13.6σ: ruled out.
- **Honest wording** (shown where results appear): one blind search reaches about V 20–21 in reflected light; the predicted Planet Nine
  is fainter (V ≥ 21; ruled out to 21.5 over most of its region by optical surveys); the one place SPHEREx adds is 3 µm (D4), with a
  small margin (1.4–1.7×). What SPHEREx gives any candidate found elsewhere: its place in three or more passes, its distance from the
  jump and a 102-channel spectrum.
- Not built: linking faint peaks by their motion (at 5σ the sum finds a place, not a track); spectral weights for icy bodies (gave
  1.00–1.03×).
